# Next Owner Market — Marketing & Growth Plan

*September 29, 2026. Goal: turn a 25,000 sq ft warehouse into steady cash flow in 90 days, then grow the marketplace so other people's stuff pays you too.*

## The one-line pitch

**"Everything in the warehouse, on your phone. Tested, priced, ready to pick up."**
For buyers: a real store with search, not a wall of Marketplace posts. For sellers: *We test it, photograph it, list it, and sell it. You get paid.*

## Who we're talking to

1. **Bargain hunters and DIYers** in Charlottesville, Lynchburg, Richmond, Roanoke (30–60 mile pickup radius). Tools, kitchen, household.
2. **Vintage audio buyers** (statewide and national, will ship). Your specialty; highest margins; most loyal repeat buyers.
3. **Resellers and flippers** who buy lots and pallets. Fewer people, bigger tickets, clears space fastest.
4. **Consignors**: people with a garage, an estate, or a parent's house full of stuff, who'd rather hand it off than deal with strangers.

## Week 1: get 50 items live, then tell people

The site needs to look stocked before you promote it. Nobody shares an empty store.

- Snap mode: 50 items minimum, best stuff first (audio, name-brand tools, small appliances that test well). Aim for 100 by day 7.
- Settings: phone number in (the "Text about this" button is your #1 conversion tool), city set, tagline set.
- Pinned post in **Virginia Pro Audio Classifieds** and every local buy/sell group you're in (follow each group's rules): *"I've got 300 pallets of surplus and I'm listing it all. Browse everything here: nextownermarket.com. Local pickup in ___, some items ship."*
- Marketplace: paste 5–10 listings a day using the app's copy button. Every one ends with the store link. Steady daily posting beats a 200-item dump.
- Surplus Web Facebook Page: switch its cover photo to a "Now open: nextownermarket.com" graphic, add the site button, pin a post.

## Week 2–4: the Facebook Group and the loop

- Create the group **Next Owner Market – Virginia Surplus Buy/Sell/Trade** (public, Buy & Sell turned on). Rules: price in every post, one post per item, no scams, local pickup preferred. Categories set up to match the app's.
- Pinned post: "Browse the full inventory (searchable): nextownermarket.com."
- Link the group to your Page in Meta Business Suite so you can schedule Page→group posts up to 29 days out. Batch a week of posts in one sitting.
- Post 3 kinds of content, not just listings: **New this week** (5 items with photos), **Just sold** (social proof), **Wanted** ("A buyer is looking for a Marantz receiver. Got one?"). The Wanted posts pull in consignors.
- Invite personally: every buyer who picks up gets "join the group, new stuff drops daily." Print a small card with the QR code to the store for pickups.

## The consignment offer (how you get other people's inventory)

Post this everywhere, and on a flyer at the warehouse:

> **Got stuff to sell but not the time?** We test it, photograph it, write the listing, sell it, and hand you the money. Audio gear, tools, appliances, collectibles, whole estates. Drop it off or we pick up. Sign up at nextownermarket.com/signup.

Targets for consignors: estate-sale leftovers, storage-unit buyers, landlords with abandoned property, retirees downsizing, other Marketplace sellers who hate the messaging. Your commission (40/30/15) is fair against consignment shops (30–50%) because you do the testing and photos nobody else does.

## Month 2–3: widen the funnel

- **Google**: every item page is already built for Google Product results. Add a Google Business Profile for the warehouse (free) with the site link and pickup hours. This is where "used turntable near me" traffic comes from.
- **Buyer alerts**: push "Alert me when it shows up" in every post. A buyer with a saved search comes back without you doing anything. Add Resend (free) so alerts email; add Twilio (~1¢/text) when the list has a few hundred people.
- **Reseller list**: offer lots and pallets to flippers first, before the public. Collect them via a "Reseller access" form (same Wanted form, note "reseller"). This clears the low-value bulk without listing it item by item.
- **Auctions**: run one a week on something desirable (a receiver, a tool set). Auctions get shared. Post the countdown link in the group. Ending Sunday evening works best.
- **Local partners**: record shops, repair guys, thrift stores, the Habitat ReStore. Ask them to send people who need something they don't have; offer them a finder's split on consignments they refer.
- **Craigslist and OfferUp**: same copy button; post the high-ticket items there too. Free reach.
- **Video**: one 30-second phone video a week walking a pallet or testing a piece of audio, posted to the group, Page, and TikTok/YouTube Shorts. This is the cheapest awareness you can buy, and it makes "tested and serviced" believable.

## What to measure (all in the app's Money page and Wanted list)

- Items listed per week (target: 100 by week 2, 300 by month 2)
- Sales per week and average sale price
- Your take after cost/fees (Money → this month)
- Consignors approved and their share of sales
- Wanted requests per week (demand signal; tells you what to buy at auction)
- Group members and Page followers (Facebook insights)

## Money later, once the group is a few hundred strong

Featured/pinned listings ($5–10/week), a verified-sellers tier, local sponsors (shops and repair people), affiliate links for supplies (Novus, Super Lube, styluses), and, further out, licensing the app itself to other surplus dealers.

## Things to avoid

- Auto-posting tools for Marketplace or Groups. Facebook has no API for either and restricts accounts that use browser bots. Copy-paste is the only safe way.
- Paying for Facebook ads before the store has 100+ items and a few sold. Organic group posting will outperform ads at this stage.
- Sharing the Vercel/Supabase/GitHub logins. Give helpers a staff account inside the app instead.
- Listing everything. Sell the good stuff individually and the rest in lots. One good receiver pays more than twenty lamps.
