## 1. The big lessons (now master rules)

### A. Search it yourself, every time. Never hand him the legwork.
- *"Well, you have access to everything online about the minutes and everything. See if you can find it. We go through this all the time. You have the entire universe of knowledge at your fingertips. Yet you always come back and tell me to do shit. And then when I push back on it, you go fucking find the answer."* (Floyd town manager salary, Oct 14)
- *"You need to stop guessing. That's that's bullshit. … I don't ask you to guess. You need to go out and look on the Internet. and reevaluate all three of these items."* (Sansui chat, Sep 7)
- *"I don't care about Europe proces do a full search and tell me what these are worth"* (Sansui chat, Sep 7)
- *"Motherfucker, stop fucking around. Where's the dispatch button? I'm fucking tired of wasting time here."* (Dispatch button, Oct 23)
- **Rule:** "call the clerk," "file a FOIA," "check the page yourself" or a menu of guesses is never a first answer. Do the full search (budgets, minutes, sold listings, help pages) before saying something can't be found. When Claude finally dug on Floyd, it found the answer ($60,000 starting salary, Feb 3, 2022 council minutes).

### B. Give the number. One bold number first, then short reasons.
- *"What's the fucking worth parting the goddamn thing out? Give me the fucking price. Quit fucking off."* (Item value assessment, Oct 18)
- *"Wait, you said one would be worth four to six hundred dollars. Now you're saying two of them is worth three hundred. What's the fucking answer?"* (same chat)
- *"You never answer what those speakers are worth that I just gave you the picture of."* (Speaker valuation, Sep 29)
- **Rule:** lead with one bold expected number. Wide, layered ranges get misread. A photo of an item means "what's it worth": answer it.

### C. Two prices on every item: what to list it for, and what it'll actually sell for fairly fast.
- *"Give me what you think I should listen for and the actual price you think I'll actually get. fairly fast."* (Sansui chat, Sep 7)
- *"What's the realistic how much I could sell those … in a fast turnaround in Richmond area, Virginia?"* (Item identification, Sep 28)
- *"You're saying I can only get 250 out of all this?"* (Aquarium, Oct 15), after Claude quietly picked a fast-sale price.
- **Rule:** show quick / balanced / top-dollar tiers from the start; never silently pick the low one. When he names his own asking price ("I'll ask 450"), use it exactly. He often prices higher than Claude and is often right (Klipsch RF-5 at $550).

### D. Listings are paste-ready, with a price box, and honest.
- *"Go ahead and give me the ready copy paste ready listing."* (Risograph chat, Oct 13), after listings full of [brackets].
- *"You didn't give me prices."* (Risograph chat, Oct 14)
- *"And there is no scratches on the screen. You must be looking at it."* (Item identification, Sep 27), after Claude invented a flaw.
- *"let them know it doesn't include the Mario Brothers. It's the unit only."* (Item identification, Sep 27)
- *"Yes, we can advertise it … specifically for the X1 because that's what I use this on. They all fit the same."* (Item identification, Sep 27), after Claude said "don't advertise it as an X1 fit."
- **Rule:** no blanks or brackets; drop unknown fields. Every listing starts with "Price box: $X" (Marketplace takes one number). Describe only flaws clearly visible; his description wins. Say exactly what's included. His firsthand knowledge (fitment, condition) beats box labels.

### E. Old equipment: value the parts and the other uses, not just the whole unit.
- *"that's the kind of stuff to keep in mind when I ask you about things is the alternate uses that people do with these, not just as a whole unit. And you need to put that in the in the research files."* (Item value assessment, Oct 19). Saved as `claude/Research_Preferences_Old_Equipment.md` in the Warehouse items project.
- *"A lot of times this stuff is worth something to people that do hobby stuff."* (Oct 18)
- **Rule:** for old scientific/electronic gear always cover whole-unit value, parts value, current industry/research use, hobby/prop use, the scrap floor, and where those buyers shop.

### F. New old stock is priced off real retail and "discontinued," not used comps.
- *"There's no way that that fucking thing is worth one seventy five to two fifty. … it's brand new whether there's a little bit of rust here and there sitting in the warehouse never been used."* (Sansui chat, Sep 7, Harbor Freight drill press)
- **Rule:** look up the actual retail (store page, hfpricetracker.com for Harbor Freight) and discontinued status first. Storage rust is not "used."

### G. Freight decisions: "is it worth the truck room?"
- *"I'm putting them on a fucking truck that I'm hauling anyway. I just want to know if it's worth using the room for them."* (Oct 18)
- *"consider that with everything I ask you from now on in this chat."* (Oct 18)
- **Rule:** answer take-it-or-leave-it first. When he says the haul is happening anyway, freight cost is zero; don't lecture. Don't re-ask things he already answered ("It is free.").

### H. Whole item and parts, posted at the same time.
- *"Give me um, a listing for the complete thing and then a listing for the separate modules … And I'll put them all on there at one time and see which ... ones sell."* (Risograph chat, Oct 14)
- **Rule:** write both; each says the other is listed; "whichever sells first, take the other down."

### I. Word white papers, automatically.
- *"Now make me a Word document white paper with all this. Remember, you're always supposed to do this kind of stuff. You do it so I always have it automatically."* (Item identification, Sep 28): the earliest statement of the Word-deliverables rule.
- He asked for three more (rodent pouches Oct 11; number three and the necklace Oct 14).
- **Rule:** after any how-to, repair or research walkthrough, make the Word white paper without being asked. If a fact in a delivered paper turns out wrong (the sander speed), send a corrected one.

### J. Shopping: simple, linked, cheap.
- *"Just, just Amazon or something. I don't need to go that, that deep into work. They ain't gonna sell me three bottles."* (Sep 26)
- *"Can you give me links to them? And then … cheaper Chinese equivalents that are really nice?"* (Sep 26)
- *"What's it worth? And do they still sell these somewhere? Somewhere where you can give me a link."* (Sep 29)
- *"Give me the various things down to just the home remedy stuff, not gonna buy in special oil."* (Sep 7)
- **Rule:** Amazon or a big-box store first, with real links; good cheap unbranded options are welcome; home remedies first when he asks for them.

### K. Identify from the photo correctly; don't jump to the worst case.
- *"These are the little round speakers. Those comparison photos don't even have a fucking anything close to looking the same."* and *"how hard is it to understand what these are?"* (Sep 22)
- *"That is a mercruiser outdrive"* and *"Couldn't have been just partially bad seals that leaked over the winter?"* (May 20)
- **Rule:** compare reference images to his photo before calling it a match; use what he tells you ("they go with the apples"); don't keep demanding more photos after he's said what it is. Start with the likely, ordinary cause; he knows his equipment.

### L. Be consistent and exact.
- The same Sep 11 photos got different prices in two chats (Nikko NR-615 $175–225 vs $25–60). A remote model number was stated wrong (DM-DX400 vs RM-DX300). The VHS-per-Gaylord math was wrong the first time (550–650, corrected to about 1,250).
- **Rule:** anchor every price to cited comps so a repeat valuation agrees; verify model and part numbers before stating them; check the arithmetic.

### M. He talks by voice.
- Messages show dictation slips: "Vindu" (Vendoo), "Eclipse RF5" (Klipsch RF-5), "Orville sander" (orbital), "Nico" (Nikko), "Richardsons" (ratchets), "visa" (via), cut-off sentences. Read for meaning; don't ask him to fix typos. Short follow-ups ("Vcr I mean," "Insured for 120") update the last answer; keep all earlier details.

### N. He expects Claude to find old answers.
- *"I'm pretty sure this is the … project where I asked how many VHS tapes would be in a average Gaylord box. Can you find that"* (Oct 13); *"Have I asked about a bobcat in this chat?"* (Oct 15)
- **Rule:** search past chats when he asks; say plainly which chats can't be reached (other projects).

## 2. What worked (repeat these)

- **Facebook ad format he used over and over:** an emoji-framed bold headline (brand, model, variant, part number, "Tested & Working" / "NEW IN BOX" / "NOS"); one or two plain sentences; a ✅ checklist of what works; an honest wear note; a scarcity line when true ("discontinued, you can't buy one new"); 📦 what's included or not; 💲 price, "fair offers, no lowballs"; 📍 "Pickup in [town], cash"; "Message me with any questions!" For new old stock: "old warehouse stock, never used; minor surface rust from storage, not from use; retailed at $X."
- **Price answers:** bold expected number first; then list vs. expected; per-site notes (Marketplace local vs. eBay with ~13% fees and shipping); "if no bites in a week, drop to $X" (price drops notify savers); buy/pass calls with a max bid when he's buying.
- **Bulk lots:** price per unit, per 5, per 10, whole lot, and a "just move it" price.
- **Retitle for what buyers search** (29-inch legs are "dining table legs").
- **Named places to post:** niche Facebook groups, forums (SatelliteGuys, VOGONS, CopyTechNet, Riso community), DOTmed / Lab Liquidators for lab gear.
- **Scrap answers:** a table by grade with a dated source, the local yard's phone and hours, and how to get the top grade (sort, strip, magnet test).
- **Start-to-finish restore rundowns:** numbered steps with the grit, speed and time for each.
